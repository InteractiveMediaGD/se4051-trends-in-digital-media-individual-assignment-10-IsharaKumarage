## What I Explored

For the self-study component of this assignment, I explored the concept of **affective computing** and **context-aware adaptive environments** — specifically, how digital systems can be designed to detect and respond meaningfully to a user's emotional state without requiring complex artificial intelligence or biometric sensors.

Affective computing, a term introduced by Rosalind Picard at MIT in 1997, refers to systems that can recognise, interpret, and simulate human emotions. While full affective computing systems often rely on physiological sensors or machine learning models, a simplified version — where the user explicitly declares their emotional state — can still produce a system that feels genuinely responsive and emotionally intelligent.

I also studied how **sensory environments** influence human psychology. Research in environmental psychology shows that lighting colour temperature and intensity significantly affect mood: cool blue tones promote calmness, warm orange tones increase energy and alertness, and bright neutral white light supports concentration. Similarly, ambient audio has well-documented effects on mental state, with nature sounds reducing cortisol levels and steady low-frequency tones improving sustained attention.

## How It Enhances the System

The Smart Relaxation Room implements a layered emotion-aware system:

**Layer 1 — Explicit emotional input:** The user selects their current mood (Stressed, Tired, or Unfocused). This is analogous to a user verbally reporting their emotional state to a system. The environment immediately adapts its lighting colour, audio, and feedback text — mapping directly to established psychological principles about sensory environments and mood regulation.

**Layer 2 — Implicit spatial behaviour:** The user's movement through the room is interpreted as contextual intent. Walking into the Relax Zone is treated as a desire for calm; the Focus Zone signals a need for concentration. This is inspired by context-aware computing, where the system infers intent from behaviour rather than requiring explicit commands.

**Layer 3 — Accessibility adaptation:** The Reduce Effects feature acknowledges that not all users respond to stimuli in the same way. Users with sensory sensitivity can request a quieter, softer version of any active mode. This reflects the human-centred design principle that adaptive systems must be inclusive.

Together, these three layers create a system that behaves intelligently — not because it uses AI, but because it applies structured knowledge about human emotional responses to produce outputs that feel relevant and considerate.
